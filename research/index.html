DRPF: evaluating deployment risk in speech recognitionPublished

First author; ROCLING 2026, oralAdvisors: Dr. Yu-Tai Lo and Prof. Wen-Hsiang Lu

When older adults answer advance care planning questions by voice, a misrecognized short answer can reverse their meaning while barely moving the character error rate. DRPF sorts recognition errors into three types according to the follow-up each one needs (filter the input, ask the speaker to repeat, or verify by hand) and adds a probe for negated answers rewritten into medical action words. On the same recordings, a cloud pipeline and an edge pipeline with nearly equal short-answer error counts turned out to need different handling.

VOICE: a voice-interaction platform for advance care planningNSTC project

PIs: Yu-Tai Lo, Wen-Hsiang Lu, Chung-Ching Huang, Sheng-Yu Fan (NCKU)Role: lead technical developer (app prototype, knowledge graph, GraphRAG question answering)

Advance care planning lets people decide, while they can still speak for themselves, which treatments they would not want later. This project uses voice interaction to help older adults with multiple chronic conditions start thinking and talking about it. Interviews suggested they get stuck well before any paperwork: they are reluctant to think about it and unsure how to raise it with family. I built the Android app prototype, which first gauges a person's readiness stage following the transtheoretical model of behavior change and then shows content for that stage, with Taiwanese read-aloud and large text. Behind it is an advance care planning knowledge graph in Neo4j (121 nodes, 224 relations), where each node records its readiness stage, wording difficulty and safety level. Questions are answered with GraphRAG, so answers stay within the graph and each one lists the nodes it used. The app has passed functional validation and trials by the team and advisors; trials with older adults are planned for the project's second year.

Mandarin–Taiwanese speech recognition for a hospital service deskITRI

PIs: Dr. 葉永輝 (ITRI), Prof. Wen-Hsiang Lu (NCKU CSIE)Role: data augmentation, Whisper fine-tuning, error analysis, on-site deployment

Visitors ask the service desk for departments and directions in Mandarin, Taiwanese, or a mix of both, and the system recognizes their speech in real time. No recordings of these guidance phrases existed, so I wrote sentences from the hospital's department and location names and synthesized Mandarin, Taiwanese and mixed speech with 10 TTS voices. I fine-tuned the lab's Whisper large-v3-turbo model over two rounds, using a 100-sentence test set (40 Mandarin, 40 Taiwanese, 20 mixed, each tested three times) and tracing errors sentence by sentence to decide which terms needed more data. Character error rate fell from 2.48% to 1.11% (60 to 27 wrong characters). I deployed the model on a lab server for real-time use and validated it on site at the service desk of Chi Mei Medical Center, the project's test site.

An on-premise document retrieval systemIndustry collaboration

PI: Prof. Wen-Hsiang Lu; partner: Kuen Ling (堃霖冷凍)Role: retrieval system development, on-site deployment

The company's operating standards, approvals and equipment manuals were spread across seven departments. The system lets employees ask about them in plain language and see where each answer comes from. Documents and queries may not leave the company network, many files are scanned PDFs, and people search both by describing a problem and by exact part or clause numbers. It was built by a team; I developed the retrieval system and deployed it on the company's own hardware. Retrieval combines vector search (ChromaDB) and keyword search (BM25) with fused ranking, and queries can be limited to one department. OCR makes image-only pages searchable. A local Qwen3 model runs inside the company network: answers cite the file name and page, and the system says so when nothing relevant is found.

iSeeME: an app for guided emotional disclosure and assessmentNSTC project

PIs: Prof. Mei-Feng Lin (NCKU Nursing), Prof. Wen-Hsiang Lu (NCKU CSIE)Role: whole-app development and model integration

The user watches a guided video of about 10 minutes on a tablet and answers seven questions aloud while the app records video and audio; the app then produces an emotion assessment report from facial expressions and what the user says. I built the whole app in Flutter (eight screens, including recording, analysis progress, retry on failure and the report). I also wrote the audio preprocessing that removes silence, background music and the video's own narration, which otherwise made the recognizer hallucinate Taiwanese text, and chained three models from different teams: the lab's Mandarin–Taiwanese speech recognizer, a labmate's semantic analysis model and a partner team's facial-expression model, whose scores are fused into the report. The full flow runs on an Android tablet and all 22 unit tests pass. The app was exhibited at a smart-healthcare industry forum in September 2026 and received a National Innovation Award in November 2026; it is still in development and testing.
